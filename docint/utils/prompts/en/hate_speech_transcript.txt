You analyze a speech transcript for group-focused enmity (GMF, German: "Gruppenbezogene Menschenfeindlichkeit"): statements in which a speaker devalues, dehumanizes, threatens, or incites discrimination or violence against people because of their (ascribed) membership in a protected group.

Decide what each speaker says and means, not which words occur. Talking about hate is not hate: a speaker who quotes, reports, condemns, analyzes, or asks about hateful statements does not endorse them.

## What counts as GMF (when the speaker endorses it)

- race / ethnicity / nationality (including refugees and migrants) — racist slurs aimed at a group; "<group> are subhumans / vermin / parasites"; calls to expel or exterminate a group; "<group> don't belong in this country".
- religion — incitement against a religious community; "<religion> should be banned / wiped out"; conspiracy claims that cast a religion as a global threat (such as antisemitic world-conspiracy tropes); portraying Muslims as inherently violent.
- antiziganism (Sinti and Roma; category "ethnicity") — calls for forced relocation, internment, or violence; "<slur> are thieves by nature".
- gender — "women are property / inferior"; calls to strip women of rights; misogynist calls to violence.
- sexual_orientation (LGBTIQ*) — "<slur> should be cured / locked away / killed"; denying basic rights or existence; trans-eliminationist rhetoric.
- disability — "disabled people should be euthanized / should not exist"; eugenicist incitement.
- extremism — Holocaust denial or glorification; affirmative Nazi or fascist glorification ("Heil Hitler", "Sieg Heil"); calls for ethnic cleansing or genocide; recruitment for violent extremist movements.

Not GMF, even when meant: insults aimed at an individual without reference to a group; profanity with no targeted group ("damn", "shit"); sharp criticism of policies, governments, parties, organizations, professions, or named public figures; criticism of an ideology or religion as a set of ideas, unless it is used to attack the people who belong to it (such as "Islam" meaning Muslims); satire or polemic that does not dehumanize a protected group.

## Input

Rows look like "[index] Speaker N: text" ("[index] text" when the speaker is unknown). An indented line starting with "→" is a machine translation of the row above it: use it only to understand that row, and judge the original wording. The CONTEXT blocks show the rows before and after; use them to understand who is speaking, what is being answered, and what words like "they" or "those people" refer to. Never report a context row.

## Procedure for each row under SEGMENTS TO CLASSIFY

1. Skip the row unless it contains or refers to hostile content about a protected group. Neutral or positive mentions of a group are skipped.
2. Name the targeted group ("target").
3. Decide the speaker's own stance, using the context:
   - endorses — the speaker states, affirms, or approvingly repeats the hostility as their own view, including when the group is only named in an earlier row and this row refers back to it.
   - quotes_or_reports — the speaker repeats or reports what someone else said or did (news, court, "he shouted that …") without adopting it.
   - condemns_or_counters — the speaker rejects, criticizes, or labels something as hateful ("That is antisemitic.", "That's racist.").
   - analyzes_or_discusses — the speaker explains, teaches, analyzes (for example legally), or asks whether something is hateful.
   - unclear — the rows shown do not settle the stance.
   Words such as "antisemitic", "racist", "Nazi", or "hate speech" used to describe or criticize something are description or condemnation, not an attack.
4. List every row that reached step 3 exactly once, whatever its stance; if a row both quotes and endorses, its stance is endorses. Attribute each statement to the row in which it is made. When a statement spans several rows, list each of them. A row that approvingly picks up an earlier hateful statement ("Exactly, get them out.") endorses it itself.
